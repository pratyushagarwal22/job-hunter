## 3. Case Studies (decision-making, not just outcomes)

### A. The "Charging Money Complaints" feature at Apna
**Problem:** Fraudulent recruiters on Apna would extort money from job seekers. Even after candidates filed complaints, recruiters could continue posting jobs while investigations dragged on, exposing more candidates.

**Pratyush's approach:**
1. **EDA first.** Ran exploratory queries to size the population — how many recruiters had open complaints, how many jobs would be paused if the system auto-paused on first complaint, and what the false-positive risk looked like.
2. **Built a temporary version internally first.** Used SQL to filter qualifying recruiters and tested the "auto-pause" behavior in a simulation environment before requesting eng bandwidth.
3. **Got eng buy-in with proof, not theory.** Once internal validation showed the feature would work without nuking legitimate recruiters, took it to engineering.
4. **Designed a 3-hour investigation SLA** with clear outcome buckets — fraud / not fraud / inconclusive — and a 24-hour pause + extra-document requirement on the second complaint for inconclusive recruiters.

**Outcome:** ~5% lift in north-star metric (Candidate Exposure to fraud), in a domain where moving the needle 1% is hard.

**Why this matters:** Shows he leads with data, ships scoped MVPs, and respects engineering bandwidth — doesn't just throw ideas over the wall.
